# Stone Set Hypertrophy Routine Baseline

Updated: 2026-08-03
Status: `ACCEPTED BASELINE`

## Purpose

This document defines the first workout-program baseline for Stone Set.

The program is designed for:

- the repository owner;
- maximal practical muscle growth rather than powerlifting specialization;
- the limited equipment verified in the supplied routine;
- five weekly resistance-training sessions;
- two full resistance-training rest days;
- a strict maximum of 60 minutes from the beginning of the warm-up to the end of the final working set.

This is an evidence-informed starting prescription, not a claim that research has identified one universally optimal routine. The program must later adapt from recorded performance, recovery, pain, and adherence data.

## Verified equipment boundary

The routine uses only equipment already present in the supplied workout plan:

- Smith machine;
- adjustable dumbbells and bench;
- cable station;
- lat-pulldown station;
- seated-row station;
- leg-extension machine;
- leg-curl machine;
- hanging-knee-raise setup.

No exercise may depend on equipment outside this boundary unless the equipment inventory is explicitly updated.

## Weekly structure

| Day | Session | Target duration |
|---|---|---:|
| Monday | Upper A | 50-58 minutes |
| Tuesday | Lower A | 52-59 minutes |
| Wednesday | Delts and forearms | 35-45 minutes |
| Thursday | No resistance training | — |
| Friday | Upper B | 52-59 minutes |
| Saturday | Lower B | 52-59 minutes |
| Sunday | No resistance training | — |

The fifth session exists to distribute work and keep the four main sessions below 60 minutes. It is not treated as inherently superior to a four-day or full-body split when weekly training volume is equal.

## Effort standard

- Smith compounds, rows, pulldowns, split squats, and hip thrusts: normally finish at `1-2 RIR`.
- Isolation exercises: normally finish at `0-2 RIR`.
- Only the final isolation set may reach technical failure.
- Do not take Smith squats or Romanian deadlifts to failure.
- A repetition counts only when range of motion and technique remain within the exercise standard.

## Progression

Use double progression.

Example for `3 x 8-12`:

1. Keep the same load until all three working sets reach 12 controlled repetitions at the prescribed RIR.
2. Increase the load by the smallest practical increment.
3. Return to the lower portion of the repetition range.
4. Build repetitions again without sacrificing range of motion or RIR.

Do not add sets merely because a session felt easy.

## Sixty-minute execution rule

1. Start a timer when the warm-up begins.
2. Limit general warm-up and movement-specific ramp sets to `5-8 minutes`.
3. Ramp sets are not working sets and must not approach failure.
4. Preserve the prescribed rest periods for the first compound movements.
5. Use antagonist accessory pairings only where explicitly permitted.
6. Stop the session at 60 minutes.
7. If equipment delays consume the session, remove the final set of the lowest-priority isolation exercise. Do not rush Smith squats, Romanian deadlifts, or bench presses to compensate.

## Adjustment rules

Run the baseline for eight weeks while recording every working set.

- Keep volume unchanged while repetitions or load are progressing and joints feel normal.
- If several lifts regress across two consecutive weeks and fatigue remains elevated, reduce working sets by approximately half for one week.
- If one muscle group shows no rep or load progression across at least three exposures while technique, effort, and recovery remain stable, add only one weekly set to that muscle group.
- If pain is provoked by a movement, stop that movement and replace it with an equipment-compatible pattern rather than training through joint pain.
- Change one programming variable at a time.

## Primary research basis

The baseline was synthesized from the supplied routine and primary resistance-training trials, including:

1. Schoenfeld et al. Higher resistance-training volume produced greater hypertrophy in trained men across several measured sites, with diminishing practical returns and greater time cost. PMID: `30153194`.
2. Refalo et al. Failure and approximately 1-2 RIR produced similar quadriceps hypertrophy, while failure caused greater repetition and velocity loss. PMID: `38393985`.
3. Schoenfeld et al. Three-minute inter-set rests outperformed one-minute rests for several strength and hypertrophy outcomes in trained men. PMID: `26605807`.
4. Iversen et al. Multi-joint supersets nearly halved session duration and produced similar body-composition changes, but slightly compromised some pulling-strength gains. PMID: `39072654`.
5. Schoenfeld et al. Low- and high-load training produced similar muscle-thickness gains when effort was high, supporting broad repetition ranges. PMID: `25853914`.
6. Lasevicius et al. Volume-equated two- and three-day muscle-training frequencies produced similar hypertrophy and strength outcomes in trained men. PMID: `31531139`.
7. Lanza et al. Bench-press training increased pectoralis major, anterior-deltoid, and triceps cross-sectional area, supporting removal of redundant Wednesday anterior-delt pressing. PMID: `39593465`.
8. Kassiano et al. Standing calf raises produced robust triceps-surae hypertrophy and greater gastrocnemius growth than seated calf raises in a within-participant trial. PMID: `38156065`.
9. Simao et al. and related exercise-order trials show that exercises performed earlier generally receive better repetition and strength performance; priority movements therefore remain first. PMID: `15705026` and `30248269`.

## Honest limitation

The research does not prove that these exact exercises, set counts, or weekly days are uniquely optimal for this user. The correct long-term routine will be determined by the user's logged response. Stone Set must eventually treat progression, recovery, time compliance, and pain as inputs for controlled program adjustment rather than endlessly replacing exercises.
